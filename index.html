4. Classification of seizures (2025 International League Against Epilepsy)

  • The 2025 classification keeps four main classes and defines 21 seizure types.
  • Focal seizures, and seizures unknown whether focal or generalised, are subdivided by the state of consciousness (preserved or impaired), judged from both awareness (the patient can recall the event) and responsiveness.
  • If consciousness cannot be judged, the parent term is used (for example, focal seizure). Focal seizures and seizures unknown whether focal or generalised can be described as with or without observable manifestations.

4.1 Clinical significance

Correct classification matters, for example because:

  • Drug choice differs between focal and generalised seizures.
  • Some medicines worsen generalised seizures.

Source: Beniczky et al., 2025b, page 1088.

4.2 Key clinical features

Epilepsy syndromes

Each epilepsy syndrome is characterised by a typical combination of:

  • Seizure types
  • Electroencephalogram findings
  • Age of onset
  • Comorbidities

Examples

  • Childhood absence epilepsy
  • Juvenile myoclonic epilepsy
  • Lennox-Gastaut syndrome: atonic seizures are often observed.
  • Dravet syndrome: genetic; linked to SCN1A sodium channel mutations.
  • Infantile epileptic spasms syndrome

Sources: Beniczky et al., 2025a; Nguyen, Dergalust and Chang, 2025.

Status epilepticus

  • Traditional definition: more than 30 minutes of either
    • Continuous seizure activity, or
    • Two or more sequential seizures without full recovery of consciousness between them (Glauser et al., 2016).
  • Severity: a critical care emergency (Glauser et al., 2016). Mortality is up to 15.5% (American College of Clinical Pharmacy, 2026).

Time points

Time pointWhat it means
5 minutesTreatment should start once a seizure has lasted 5 minutes; treatment protocols use this point because a seizure lasting more than 5 minutes is likely to be prolonged.
30 minutesConvulsive status epilepticus of this duration may by itself cause permanent neuronal injury; after 30 minutes, status epilepticus can have long-term consequences, including neuronal death, neuronal injury and alteration of neuronal networks, depending on the type and duration of seizures.

Sources: American College of Clinical Pharmacy, 2026; Glauser et al., 2016.
